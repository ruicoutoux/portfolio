01 The Problem

OutSystems has a comprehensive and complex identity and authentication system, required to provide customers with flexible and customisation options, without compromising security. The Identity system which is a core part of the platform. A proposal was made to optimize the cost and efficiency of the Identity area, but it overlooked the product impacts on the user and customer experience.

The core pain: Moving the Identity and Authentication system would result in a cheaper architecture to maintain, but it would also break the native experience for customers, and potentially impact the ARR of the company. The decision was made without a full understanding of the product and customer impacts.

Exceptional UX is one of the OutSystems advantages

02 Constraints & Context

While acting as the SME and DRI for Identity, when I became aware of the impacts, I took the initative to immediately gather context and evidence to present to the Director Architect and Principal Architect. I gathered information on the real impact of the proposal, both concerning the product positioning, and the affected customers and potential ARR at risk, based on internal data. While operating under tight time constraints, as the decision was already in progress, I had to quickly gather evidence and convey it to the decision makers. This was a critical moment, as the decision could have had a significant negative impact on customers and the company's revenue, and at the same time negatively impact the user experience.

Different concept live at different abstraction levels

The critical constraint: This was an architectural decision, meaning that UX was not the only factor in the decision-making process. The decision makers had to consider other factors, such as cost, efficiency, and technical feasibility. Therefore, I had to present my findings in a way that would resonate with the decision makers and highlight the importance of maintaining the native experience for customers.

03 The approach

Operating under tight time constraints, the approach was to gather evidence for a short and impactful message. The goal was to present both the impact in ARR, and the in the experience, so that target audience could see both sides of the impact.

06 Outcome

Praised by the principal architect for bringing his awareness to the problem, and making him aware of the potential negative impacts on customers and ARR, the decision was reverted, preventing potential negative impacts on customers and ARR, in favor of keeping the ideal experience for the customers.
