01. The Executive Proposal: IdP Decommissioning Risk

In an effort to trim engineering maintenance overhead, executive leadership proposed deprecating the platform’s native built-in Identity Provider (IdP) in favor of strictly requiring third-party enterprise providers (Okta, Azure AD, Ping).

While logically sound on paper, this proposal ignored critical customer deployment topologies. Hundreds of high-value enterprise accounts relied on the native IdP for isolated, air-gapped, or hybrid-cloud environments where third-party SAML/OIDC federations were prohibited or technically unavailable.

⚡

Critical Architectural Trade-off: IdP Maintenance Overhead vs. Enterprise Churn Risk

Advocated for accepting short-term native IdP maintenance overhead to prevent massive long-term enterprise customer churn.

The Tension: Decommissioning native identity would save ~15% in platform engineering maintenance, but would force top-tier enterprise clients into expensive architecture rewrites or platform migration.

The Resolution: As Lead UX DRI, I spearheaded a multi-departmental audit combining customer interview evidence with precise ARR exposure calculations, presenting an unassailable financial case to the Executive Board.
